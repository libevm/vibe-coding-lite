# Vibe Coding Lite — Overview

## What is this?

A single-page tutorial website that teaches people how to start vibe coding.

Not a course. Not a framework. Not a philosophy lecture.

A clear, calm, beautiful reference you can open, scan, and use.

## Who is it for?

People who have heard about vibe coding but feel overwhelmed by the noise.

Developers, founders, and curious builders who want a starting point — not a rabbit hole.

They don't need to know everything. They need to know what to do first, second, third.

## What does it do?

It presents the vibe coding guide as a navigable, digestible experience.

Each step is its own moment — paced so you absorb one idea before meeting the next.

The information is structured, scannable, and beautiful. You can reference it mid-project without losing your place.

## What does it NOT do?

- It does not teach programming
- It does not sell a tool or framework
- It does not overwhelm with options
- It does not require signup, installation, or commitment
- It does not try to be comprehensive — it tries to be clear

## Design Intent

The website should feel like opening a well-made notebook — not a dashboard, not a docs site, not a landing page.

### Core principles

**Radical clarity** — Every element earns its place. Nothing decorative without function.

**Cognitive pacing** — Information arrives at the speed of understanding. Steps breathe. Sections have rhythm.

**Signal over noise** — Strip everything that doesn't help someone take the next step.

**Knowledge scaffolding** — Each section builds on the last. You never feel dropped into the middle.

**Calm interface** — Quiet typography, generous whitespace, no visual urgency. The content speaks.

**Playful seriousness** — The tone is warm but precise. Not corporate, not casual. Confident and human.

**Information hierarchy** — Your eye knows where to go. Headings, body, details — each has a clear visual weight.

**Humanized data** — When structure is shown (state diagrams, log examples, checklists), it feels approachable, not clinical.

### Inspirations

- Edward Tufte — analytical minimalism, every pixel carries meaning
- Giorgia Lupi — data humanism, warmth inside structure
- Moritz Stefaner — computational beauty, elegant complexity made simple
- Kim Rees — data UX, information that respects the reader
- Nicholas Felton — quantified storytelling, narrative through structure

### Feel

The site should feel:

- Snappy — instant transitions, no waiting
- Quiet — no competing for attention
- Trustworthy — the design says "someone cared about this"
- Referenceable — you come back to it mid-project and find what you need in seconds

## Success

Someone opens the site, reads for five minutes, and knows exactly how to start their next project with vibe coding.

They bookmark it. They come back. They share it with a friend who asks "what is vibe coding?"

That's it.
